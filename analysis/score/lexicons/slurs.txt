# Slurs used in the United States: exact, high-precision surface forms for three groups.
#
# Ethnic slurs. Primary source: Wikipedia, "List of ethnic slurs" (retrieved 2026-10-10). A row is
# in scope when its "Location or origin" lists the United States, North America, worldwide use, or
# a US speech community, or when its location is blank and its targets or notes tie it to American
# usage.
# Sexual-orientation and gender-identity slurs. Primary source: Wikipedia, Category:Homophobic
# slurs and Category:LGBTQ-related slurs (retrieved 2026-10-10).
# Disability slurs. Primary source: Wikipedia, "List of disability-related terms with negative
# connotations" and Category:Slurs related to low intelligence (retrieved 2026-10-10). Only terms
# used as slurs for disabled people are in scope; descriptive words with negative connotations
# (blind, handicapped) and former clinical labels now used as general insults (idiot, moron) are not.
#
# All three are cross-checked against Kaggle "Profanities in English"
# (konradb/profanities-in-english-collection); real spellings missing from Wikipedia were added,
# while leetspeak and obfuscated spellings were not, because they do not occur in the printed
# Record. Gendered insults (bitch, whore, slut) are counted as profanity, not here.
# Only forms whose main meaning is the slur are scored. Forms that are also ordinary words,
# names, places, products, or legal/historical/clinical terms are in slurs_ambiguous_audit.txt or
# are not matched; slurs_provenance.tsv records the decision for every source row. Verb uses of
# "retard", "faggot(s) of" bundles, "homo sapiens"-type phrases, and line-break fragments are
# excluded in scorers.py.
# Occurrence never establishes endorsement: the Record quotes, condemns, and reads in slurs.
