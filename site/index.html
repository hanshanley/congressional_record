Congressional activity and language

Who speaks, sponsors bills, sees sponsored bills advance, and uses profanity on the floor. Rankings are descriptive and use official Congressional Record and Bill Status data.

Language on the floor

Three transparent lexical measures are shown separately: profanity, personal hostility or disrespect, and misconduct allegations. They describe language in attributed floor remarks; they do not establish intent or whether an allegation is true.

What is shown

The charts show monthly House and Senate rates for three separate lexical measures, plus the highest-rate members in Congress 119. Rates are hits per 100,000 attributed spoken words.

What is being examined

The trend panels examine how the chamber-wide rates change over time. The member panels compare speakers with at least 25,000 words.

What the data says

House has the higher aggregate profanity rate (5.8 versus 3.2 per 100,000 words). Mr. McGOVERN has the highest eligible profanity rate at 71.9 per 100,000 words. Senate has the higher aggregate personal hostility / disrespect rate (3.2 versus 2.0 per 100,000 words). Ms. DUCKWORTH has the highest eligible personal hostility / disrespect rate at 20.2 per 100,000 words. Senate has the higher aggregate misconduct allegations rate (21.5 versus 13.9 per 100,000 words). Mr. MURPHY has the highest eligible misconduct allegations rate at 149.4 per 100,000 words.

What cannot be concluded

These are descriptive word-pattern counts, not judgments about intent. Misconduct language does not prove misconduct, and quoted profanity is excluded from a speaker's rate.

How to read these rankings

  • Speech counts include only remarks attributable to a specific member by Bioguide ID; procedural speech, submitted Extensions of Remarks, and material printed into the Record are excluded.
  • Profanity uses a narrow, hand-curated list. Passages marked as quotations are excluded from a member's rate and retained as a separate audit count.
  • Members below the word threshold are omitted from the profanity ranking because rates computed from small samples are unstable.
  • Legislative counts cover sponsored House and Senate bills (H.R. and S.) only; cosponsorships, resolutions, and amendments are outside this version.
  • A sponsored bill passing or becoming law is descriptive. It does not establish that one member personally caused the outcome or measure legislative effectiveness by itself.
  • The Congressional Record is lightly edited rather than verbatim, and source metadata can be corrected after publication.
